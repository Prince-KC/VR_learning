Acid-Base Reaction

A classic neutralization experiment showing how an acid and a base react to form salt and water.

1 Materials

  • Dilute hydrochloric acid (HCl)
  • Sodium hydroxide solution (NaOH)
  • Phenolphthalein indicator
  • Beaker, dropper and stirring rod
  • Safety goggles and gloves

2 Steps

  1. Pour a small amount of NaOH solution into a clean beaker.
  2. Add 2–3 drops of phenolphthalein — the solution turns pink.
  3. Slowly add HCl drop by drop while gently stirring.
  4. Continue until the pink color just disappears.
  5. Record the volume of HCl used.

3 Observation & Result

The pink color of the basic solution disappears as the acid neutralizes it. This shows that an acid and a base react to form a neutral solution.

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H2O

Conclusion: Acids and bases neutralize each other to form salt and water. This is called a neutralization reaction.
